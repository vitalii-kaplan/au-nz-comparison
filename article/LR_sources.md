# Literature Review Sources: Australia–New Zealand Student Visa Decision Comparison

## Study context

The present study compares publicly reported student-visa approval and refusal decisions in Australia and New Zealand during 2022–2025. It uses harmonized origin-country labels, a common decision denominator (approvals/grants plus declines/refusals), and 173 matched origin countries. It examines aggregate approval levels, paired country-level differences, concordance, country-specific tests, and origin-standardized contrasts. The study is observational: its estimates do not identify causal destination effects or individual applicant approval probabilities.

**Scope of this bibliography:** Peer-reviewed publications prioritizing visa decisions and administrative records, followed by international student mobility, policy, and Australia/New Zealand context. The local full texts listed below were inspected on 26 October 2026, and the cited bibliographic metadata was checked against their title pages. Their proximity to this study varies, so contextual articles are not presented as direct methodological predecessors. Citation counts have not been consistently established for this set.

This file is the retained human-readable source registry for the journal articles cited in the six argument-led Literature Review paragraphs. It records stable identifiers, publication details, relevance, manuscript use, and interpretation boundaries. The numeric citations used by the manuscript are generated from `bibliography.bib`. Statistical references are documented separately in `Methods-citations.md`.

## 1. Closest peer-reviewed literature

### 1.1 Visa Policy and International Student Migration: Evidence from Canada

- **Publication:** *Journal of Economic Geography*, 2024/2025.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jeg/lbae034
- **Relevance:** Very high. Uses administrative evidence to examine student-visa approval rates, application volumes, and differences by origin country in the context of a Canadian policy reform, using a triple-difference design.
- **Use in review:** Closest identified substantive and quantitative precedent. Distinguish its policy-intervention design from the present descriptive and origin-matched AU–NZ comparison.

### 1.2 The Student Migration Transition: An Empirical Investigation into the Nexus Between Development and International Student Migration

- **Publication:** *Comparative Migration Studies*, 2023.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s40878-023-00329-0
- **Relevance:** High for quantitative origin–destination analysis. Examines outgoing student mobility and destination patterns using UNESCO data covering 2003–2018.
- **Use in review:** Provides context for origin–destination comparisons. Its outcome is mobility, **not** student-visa approval decisions.

### 1.3 Examining the Impacts of Visa Policy Changes on International Student Enrolment and Mobility

- **Authors:** Rebwar Kamal Gharib, Sally Dibb, Crystal Zhang.
- **Publication:** *Studies in Higher Education*, 2026.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2026.2673034
- **Source:** https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/examining-the-impacts-of-visa-policy-changes-on-international-stu/
- **Relevance:** High thematic relevance. Multi-country visa-policy comparison involving the UK, US, Canada, and Australia.
- **Use in review:** Compare the role of visa policy in enrolment and mobility, while emphasizing that the present study examines observed visa-decision proportions rather than enrolment outcomes.

### 1.4 ISM Policy Pervasion: Visas, Study Permits, and the International Student Experience

- **Author:** Max Crumley-Effinger.
- **Publication:** *Journal of International Students*, 2024.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.32674/jis.v14i1.5347
- **Relevance:** Qualitative evidence involving international students in Australia, Canada, and the United States.
- **Use in review:** Explains why visa and study-permit administration is a meaningful component of international student mobility. Not a quantitative approval-rate comparison.

### 1.5 International Student Mobility to Canada and New Zealand: “Edugration” or “Transience”?

- **Authors:** Conrad King, Catherine Gomes, William Shannon, Ruonan Lu.
- **Publication:** *Comparative and International Education*, 2024.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5206/cie-eci.v53i2.17001
- **Relevance:** Mixed-methods comparison of students in Canada and New Zealand, considering temporary study and migration pathways.
- **Use in review:** New Zealand and comparative migration-policy context. Does not measure country-specific visa approval rates.

### 1.6 International Higher Education and Post-Study Work Rights in Australia, Germany and Canada: Strengths and Vulnerabilities

- **Authors:** Anumoni Joshi and Christopher John Ziguras.
- **Publication:** *Journal of Studies in International Education*, online 2023 / issue 2024.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/10283153231172016
- **Relevance:** Comparative analysis of post-study work policies across three destinations.
- **Use in review:** Situates international education within immigration-policy systems. Its outcome and policy focus differ from approval decisions.

### 1.7 Australian Policy on International Students: Pivoting Towards Discourses of Diversity?

- **Publication:** *The Australian Educational Researcher*, 2023.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s13384-022-00532-5
- **Relevance:** Australian international-education policy and diversity/recruitment discourse.
- **Use in review:** Australian policy background, not an explanation established by the AU–NZ decision data.

### 1.8 International Student Mobility: Pacific Islander Experiences of Higher Education in Australia

- **Publication:** *Asia Pacific Viewpoint*, 2022.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/apv.12342
- **Relevance:** Experiences of students from Pacific Island origins in Australian higher education.
- **Use in review:** Context for heterogeneous origin-country experiences, not an administrative rate comparison.

### 1.9 From Bad to Worse: Examining the Deteriorating Labour Market Outcomes of International Graduates in Australia

- **Publication:** *Journal of Population Research*, 2022.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s12546-022-09291-7
- **Relevance:** Post-study labor-market outcomes and education-to-migration pathways in Australia.
- **Use in review:** More distant context; include only if needed to explain the wider education–migration relationship.

## 3. Non-journal sources: data and policy context only

These should **not** be counted as peer-reviewed journal articles.

### NSW Universities Audit 2024

- **Source:** Audit Office of New South Wales.
- **URL:** https://www.audit.nsw.gov.au/our-work/reports/universities-2024
- **Reported contextual observation:** Average student-visa rejection rates at NSW universities rose from 4.0% in 2021 to 13.8% in 2024, according to the preceding search.
- **Caution:** Different population and measurement from the present nationwide AU–NZ comparison. Do not directly equate these rates.

## 4. Implemented literature-review structure

The review uses six paragraphs to develop five ideas; the fourth is divided into a literature-facing premise and the present study's response:

1. Visa administration is part of the structure of international student mobility, not an external procedural detail.
2. The administrative pathway begins before enrolment and remains connected to study, work, post-study rights, and migration after graduation.
3. Immigration-policy changes can affect applications, approvals, enrolment, and the composition of the observed student population.
4. Differences between destinations cannot be reduced to applicant-origin labels, although the present design does not identify a specific policy mechanism.
5. The study establishes the magnitude and heterogeneity of matched-origin decision differences while leaving causal policy explanation outside its scope.

The statistical foundations are presented in Methods rather than repeated in the Literature Review.

## 5. Suggested contribution paragraph

> Previous research has examined international student mobility, visa-policy changes, and the relationship between immigration regulation and enrolment. The present study contributes a matched-origin comparison of publicly reported student-visa decisions in Australia and New Zealand during 2022–2025, distinguishing aggregate approval levels, country-level heterogeneity, and origin-standardized differences.

**Do not claim this is the first study** without a sufficiently comprehensive and documented search. The Canadian administrative-data paper is an important close precedent.

## 8. Evidence limitations

This is a verified source set for the present narrative review, not a completed systematic review. Some items have only contextual relevance, and no uniform citation-count verification was performed. The manuscript therefore limits its gap statement to the publications reviewed here and does not claim to be the first study of its kind.
